# Reference: the 2026 film (concept and vibe only, never copied)

Researched 2026-10-01 by the scout agent from text sources (no images viewed or stored). Use this to understand the feeling we are after and, just as important, what NOT to copy. Our IP rule (CLAUDE.md): no characters, creature designs, organizations, logos, music, dialogue, or specific shots from the film or the web series.

## What the film does (summary in our words)
- The place copies reality imperfectly; each copy loses fidelity. Wrongness follows strict rules. Reported examples: furniture fused from two pieces, walls clipping through furniture, objects half sunk into floors, a misremembered pool area.
- Light: hundreds of real 4000 K tube fixtures, camera at 4300 K, yellow printed into the wallpaper itself, then a mild LUT shift. Flat, monotonous, bright enough to reveal emptiness. No shallow depth of field, no dramatic key light. Off-center, sometimes tilted framing.
- Two image layers: clean cinema for the world, and camcorder footage that was graded first and then printed to real VHS so tape damage lands last.
- Set dressing anchored in 1990s consumer culture (estate sales, hotel liquidators). Repetition used as structure (many identical chairs). Short walls and hard corners cut sightlines.
- Threat: degraded human copies, held back; reviewers found the big late creature the weakest card.

## Too close to the film: avoid or make clearly ours
- The film's protagonist is a furniture store owner and its gateway is in the store basement. Stewart's fixed decision (our game starts in a furniture store after closing, through a duct-taped wall in the back) stays, but every specific differs: you are a night closer, not the owner; the gate is a taped section of showroom wall, not a basement; our store has its own name and identity; no therapist character.
- The film's extraction team (a former MRI company in hazmat suits) is one of its organizations: do not reproduce. Our own organization is Halden Duplicating (DECISIONS D010).
- The film's threats are degraded human copies. Our Entity (M4) must not be that; see VISION.
- The film's "the place copies your store" premise: our twist is generation loss of recordings instead (DECISIONS D009).

## Transferable principles (adapted, in our words)
1. Every space is a lossy copy, and the losses follow rules the player can learn.
2. Fusion and clipping read as memory failure when they are consistent and deliberate, not as bugs.
3. Fidelity is a dial: early spaces are almost right, deeper ones lose detail, scale and function.
4. Anchor in a real, dated, mundane place, then spoil one thing.
5. Believable, monotonous light from real-looking fixtures; dread from nowhere to hide.
6. Bake color into materials, not only the grade.
7. Two image layers: clean world, degraded recording device applied last.
8. Short walls and hard corners keep emptiness threatening.
9. Repetition (the same chair, the same room) as architecture and as a sign of a copy.
10. Hold the threat back; the space carries the fear.
