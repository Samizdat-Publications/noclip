# Art direction notes from Stewart's research (S02, 2026-10-01)

Stewart shared research on what made the 2026 film's Backrooms work, plus a film still (a long, evenly lit yellow corridor, a line of ceiling panels receding to a vanishing point, a small figure standing at the far end). He said: use it as art direction, not as rules. We do not copy the film's shots, characters or story. What we take:

1. Vanishing point suppression: long, perfectly regular corridors whose repeating ceiling grid, light line and wallpaper make distance unreadable. -> "highway" corridors that run straight through many chunks (Level0Gen), lights in one line.
2. Loops without visible portals: geometry that quietly violates Euclid (a corridor that repeats; four right turns that do not bring you back). -> the loop corridor (seamless teleport), later the four-turn loop.
3. Scale incongruity: spaces copied at the wrong scale (a room whose ceiling is three times too high, doors slightly too small). -> generation-loss scale errors.
4. Lens: the camcorder view gets a period wide-angle barrel distortion in its 4:3 frame.
5. Analog degradation as memory decay: already our generation-loss twist (D009).
6. Presence at the end of the corridor: something standing far away at the end of a long hall. Ours: a figure-shaped dropout that exists only on tape (VISION: the Entity is fully there only through the camcorder).
7. Functional erasure: less purposeful infrastructure. We keep a few outlets (VISION: outlets with no purpose) and exit signs (critics' favorite beat, and our Seam language), but no thermostats, vents or switches.
8. Lighting: the film's lit corridors are diffuse and nearly shadowless. Our critics punished flatness, so lit areas stay soft and diffuse while darkness comes from dead panels and unlit side rooms (D013, L032).
