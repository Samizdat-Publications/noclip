## Best and worst shot
Best: option 5. A dark shape at the far end of a lit corridor with fog around it. You cannot tell what it is, and the hallway does the work. This is the only frame where I feel the "something lives in here" dread, and it is the one that survives repeat chases.

Worst: option 4 (camcorder, close). It is a crop of a shiny torso and chin, and it reads as a glossy plastic or latex body. The wet specular highlights on the chest and shoulders look like an oiled bodybuilder statue, and the seam lines across the chest look like a costume. The VHS filter does not hide it, and the frame is not scary.

Option 3 (close) is the runner-up for worst. The face is a good idea: calm, closed eyes, long, almost sleeping. But the plastic skin and the huge soft cast shadow on the left wall make it a statue. It is the concept art's face on a mannequin's body.

## What is wrong with it (ranked, max 5)
1. The skin is glossy plastic in every close shot (3, 4, 6). The hard specular highlights on the head, shoulders and hands look like latex or wet clay. I want matte, damp, slightly translucent skin with subsurface scatter, visible veins, pores and dirt from the carpet. As it stands it is a mannequin, and a mannequin is not scary.
2. It floats and has no weight. In options 1 and 2 the knees hover and nothing touches the carpet: the hands and knees show no contact shadow or pressure, and in 1 it looks pasted into the hall, with a fog halo around the body. In 3 the legs end in tiny feet that dangle behind it. I want knees and palms with real contact shadows, the carpet pressed down, and a slow, heavy gait.
3. The body is wrong for "too big for the corridor". In option 3 it fits the corridor neatly and the arms are placed symmetrically like a push-up. A giant crawling through a space too small would be squeezed: shoulders scraping the walls, back against the ceiling tiles, limbs folded wrong. Option 6 is much better here, with the long reaching arm and the bent posture. I want that asymmetry in every shot.
4. The hands in option 6 are good, long and wrong, but the clean tapered fingernails and the smooth surface look sculpted rather than alive. The same goes for the clean seam on the shoulders and chest (3, 4, 6), which looks like a suit zipper. Remove the seam, add wrinkles and slackness.
5. The camcorder view (2, 4) adds almost nothing, and option 4 hurts. A static closeup of a chest is just anatomy. What I want through the lens is the thing I could not see without it: a face that is closer than it should be, an extra limb, or the head tilting to follow me. The camera should be the reveal, not another angle of the same model. Also, in option 1 the head is a blown-out white sphere that reads as a lamp, not a face.

## Keep
- The corridor and lighting: yellow wallpaper, the ceiling tiles, the single bright panel and the hum-and-fog atmosphere. They are the best thing in these shots, and option 5 works because of them.
- The dark far-distance silhouette (option 5), and the tension of never seeing it clearly until it is near.
- The calm, closed-eyed face of option 3. A creature this big that does not look angry is much worse than a snarling one.
- The long, reaching, wrong arms of option 6, and its low angle with the huge hand taking over the frame. It is the strongest "caught" frame.
- The size contrast with the corridor: it should still barely fit.
