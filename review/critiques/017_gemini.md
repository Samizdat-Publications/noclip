# Player Review

## Gut reaction
At first glance, I rolled my eyes because it looks like yet another generic Backrooms asset flip. Visually, it's incredibly flat and dated. But the moment the camcorder came out, I was hooked. The mechanic of using the camera as a "lens of truth" to see hidden portals and invisible entities is fantastic, and the audio design is doing massive heavy lifting to create tension. If the graphics get an overhaul to match the quality of the concept and sound, this could be a sleeper hit.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The lighting is completely baked and flat. Look at the first high-res still: the ceiling lights don't cast pools of light on the floor or walls, making the whole environment look like a PS2 game. You need stark, harsh fluorescent lighting.
2. [hurts] Lack of contact shadows and ambient occlusion. Look at the grey partition in the second high-res still—it looks like it's floating above the carpet because there's no shadow grounding it.
3. [hurts] The materials don't match your pitch. The store page says "carpet that never dries," but this is completely matte, dry office carpet. It needs specular highlights and roughness variation to look damp.
4. [hurts] The ceiling is just a flat textured plane. The fluorescent light fixtures need to be actual 3D geometry recessed into the grid to give the space believable depth.
5. [hurts] Performance is shockingly heavy for how it looks. The dual-rendering setup for the camcorder is likely tanking your framerate. Hitting only 75 FPS on a high-end 50-series laptop GPU means lower-end rigs will crawl.
6. [nitpick] The yellow wallpaper tiling is too obvious. You can see the exact same smudge repeating every few feet down the hallway. 

## Working, don't touch
- The "lens of truth" camcorder mechanic. Seeing the chalk outline with your eyes (Still 3) and the glowing portal through the camera (01:41.500) is a brilliant gameplay loop.
- The audio design. The constant, droning room tone combined with the violent, aggressive static when the monster appears (00:38.500) is perfect. 
- The visual glitching on the camcorder when the entity is near. It obscures the monster just enough to keep it scary.

## Steam review one-liner
A brilliant, terrifying camcorder mechanic trapped inside a visually flat Backrooms clone.

## What I want next
An overhaul of the lighting and materials to make the space feel physically real, and the ability to actually step through one of those glowing camera-only portals.
