# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This is a Backrooms game with a genuinely good hook: a camcorder that acts as a Lens of Truth, revealing hidden doors and entities. The mechanic of having to look through the grainy viewfinder to navigate impossible spaces is tense and works well. But the visual polish is severely lacking right now. It feels like a great prototype trapped inside placeholder art, especially that completely untextured camera model you have to stare at constantly.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The camcorder model in normal view (Frame 3) looks like a blockout mesh. It's just flat, untextured grey polygons. For a game centered around this object, it needs to look like a tactile, scratched-up piece of 90s tech, not a placeholder.
2. [hurts] The wall stains and dirt (Frame 1) look like flat image overlays. They don't interact with the wallpaper's bump map or the lighting, making them look like stickers pasted on the wall.
3. [hurts] Normal vision lighting is too uniform. The backrooms should feel harshly lit directly under the fluorescents and unnervingly dark in the corners. Right now, it's just evenly bright everywhere, which kills the dread before you even raise the camera.
4. [nitpick] The transition when walking through a camera-only door is an instant hard cut. Adding a slight visual tear, static burst, or spatial warp would make crossing the threshold feel more impactful.

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic of the camera revealing a different reality. It's executed perfectly and instantly understandable.
- The sound design. The heavy click of bringing the camera up and the building drone when the entity is on screen are fantastic.
- The UI of the camcorder view (battery, REC, timestamp) feels authentic without cluttering the screen.

## Steam review one-liner
A Backrooms game that actually has a clever gimmick with its reality-bending camcorder, but it needs a serious graphics overhaul to hide the placeholder assets.

## What I want next
Puzzles that force me to navigate a room using only the camera's screen because the path doesn't exist in normal vision, and a fully textured camcorder model.
