# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This hits the Backrooms liminal space aesthetic perfectly. The endless yellow wallpaper, the oppressive lighting, and the squishy carpet are exactly what I expect, but the real hook is the camcorder mechanic. Pulling the camera up to reveal a glowing, impossible doorway that isn't there to the naked eye is a fantastic "wow" moment. It immediately makes me want to scan every empty room to see what else reality is hiding from me.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The shadow cast by the tripod in the camcorder view (Frame 04, end of video) is horribly pixelated and stair-stepped. It looks like a super low-resolution shadow map and instantly breaks the immersion.
2. [hurts] The doorframes (Frame 01, Frame 03) look incredibly cheap. The wood texture is flat, repeating, and looks like a placeholder asset from 2004. They desperately need better materials or actual geometric depth.
3. [nitpick] The lighting in the long yellow hallways (Frame 03) feels too even and flat. The ceiling light panels don't seem to cast convincing, distinct pools of light and shadow on the walls or floor.
4. [nitpick] The transition when walking through the hidden glowing seam in the video feels a bit abrupt. A slight audio cue or visual warp as you cross the threshold would sell the "impossible space" better.

## Working, don't touch
- The audio design is doing a lot of heavy lifting. The oppressive, never-ending hum and the squishy footsteps are exactly what this space should sound like.
- The camcorder mechanic. Using it as a lens to see hidden reality (the glowing doorway) is brilliant and gives the player an active way to engage with the environment.
- The VHS filter on the camcorder view. It has just the right amount of scanlines and distortion without blinding the player or making it frustrating to navigate.

## Steam review one-liner
Finally, a liminal space game that gives you a reason to look closer instead of just running away.

## What I want next
More impossible architecture and secrets that can only be interacted with through the camcorder—like invisible bridges, hidden messages on the walls, or shifting room layouts.
