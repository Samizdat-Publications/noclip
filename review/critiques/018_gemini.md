# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It's another Backrooms game, but the camcorder mechanic genuinely elevates it. Having to look through the lens to see hidden doors or the entity adds a fantastic layer of paranoia to the exploration. The atmosphere is thick, the lighting is harsh and realistic, and the sound design makes you feel completely isolated. However, the monster itself is incredibly disappointing right now—it looks like a random NPC spawned in time-out, which completely shatters the tension.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The monster design (00:19.500). It's just a regular dude in a grey shirt and jeans facing the wall like he's in Blair Witch time-out. In a surreal, impossible space, the threat needs to be unnatural or uncanny, not a guy waiting for a bus.
2. [hurts] The "glitch" attack (01:28.500). The entity just sort of static-teleports toward the player in a straight line. It feels like a cheap jumpscare placeholder compared to the slow, creeping dread of the rest of the game. 
3. [nitpick] The transition to the dark room (00:27.500). The lighting suddenly cuts to pitch black down the hallway. It feels like stepping across a harsh lighting volume boundary rather than a natural falloff into darkness.
4. [nitpick] The footstep sounds are a bit sharp and repetitive. They sound more like walking on hard dirt or thin rugs rather than the squishy, never-drying carpet described in the pitch.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder reveal mechanic. Seeing the glowing duct-tape door (00:06.000) only through the lens is a brilliant hook that forces players to use the camera.
- The UI and visual treatment of the camcorder view. The REC text, subtle static, and focal shifts are perfect.
- The audio design for the fluorescent lights. That hum is perfectly balanced to be maddening but not deafening.
