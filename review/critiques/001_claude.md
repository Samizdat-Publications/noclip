# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This is a very clean Backrooms-style yellow office maze that looks better than most asset-flip versions of the idea. It is also a hundred screenshots of the same hallway. I got 21 frames and four sheets with no monster, no props, no chalk marks, no footprints, no dropped camera and nothing impossible. The store page promises "rooms nobody built" and "the hallway behind you was shorter a minute ago." What I see is a plain, empty, well-lit office. Performance is bad for what's on screen. I'd wishlist it, not buy it.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] Nothing is happening. Across 21 frames and 4 sheets there is no creature, no mark, no prop, no sign of a previous death and no impossible geometry. The pitch sells chalk, footprints and a dropped camera, and I see none of it. I wanted something to look at in every other frame, even a small oddity such as a wrong door, a wall that didn't match, or a footprint trail.
2. [hurts] The space doesn't feel impossible yet. frame_14 and frame_12 are large and symmetrical but just a bigger office. The door in the open room (frame_21, sheet_01) leads to another normal room. I wanted at least one moment where the layout contradicts itself, like a corridor longer than the room it sits in, or a door that opens onto the room I just left.
3. [hurts] The whole picture is one yellow-brown. Walls, ceiling, carpet, even the camcorder tint, which just pushes it browner (frame_16, frame_20). Nothing breaks it, so the screenshots blur together. I wanted a little contrast, such as a cold fluorescent that doesn't match, one off-colour room, or a stain that isn't beige.
4. [hurts] It runs poorly for what it shows. 38.9 fps with 1% lows near 26 is not acceptable on that hardware at 1440p in empty rooms, and horror needs smooth camera turns. I'd refund over this once the novelty wore off.
5. [hurts] Shadow edges look like low-res cutouts. The shadow on the left wall in frame_04 (around x330-340, y560-780) has stair-stepped teeth. The floor shadow in frame_10 has the same problem, and so does the floor edge on the right in frame_19. The sharp darkness cut-offs also look odd. The dark half of frame_05 looks like a void rather than a lit room.
6. [nitpick] The glossy floor puddles in sheet_03 read as odd blobs of light. They look like decals sliding on the carpet instead of reflections. Elsewhere the carpet looks matte, so the shine looks inconsistent.
7. [nitpick] The camcorder frame in frame_07 is so dark and noisy it is just a mud-brown rectangle with a pillar in it. Dark should be scary, but this is simply unreadable. In frame_06 the REC dot is missing (blinking, presumably). Horizontal tearing near the door edge in frame_02 and the wall edges in frame_03 looks like a distortion shader, but I can't tell if it is intentional.

## Working, don't touch
- The ceiling is the best-looking part. Stained tiles, missing tiles showing black holes (frame_04, frame_09), and fly-speck diffusers that glow without looking like stickers (frame_01, frame_15, frame_19).
- Wallpaper and baseboard detail hold up close, and the stripes and dots on the wallpaper in frame_20 and frame_09 sell the "forgotten office" feel.
- The camcorder HUD (REC, battery bars, 1996 date and timestamp) is a good framing device and fits the setting.
- The big pillar room (frame_14, frame_08) has real scale and a good sense of depth.
- The wall-bounce glow on the walls near ceiling lights (frame_13, sheet_03) is lovely and subtle. Keep it.

## What I want next
- One real impossible-space moment I can screenshot, something the camcorder shows that my eyes don't, or a room that is wrong once I turn around.
- Something alive or leftover in the space, like the chalk marks, footprints and dropped camera the store page promised, so I feel watched and not just alone in an empty office.
