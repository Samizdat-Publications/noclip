# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I am honestly exhausted by the Backrooms aesthetic, and my first instinct was to roll my eyes at the exact same yellow wallpaper and fluorescent lights I've seen in fifty other games. However, the camcorder mechanic actually hooked me. Using the camera to reveal hidden doors and impossible architecture turns a played-out walking simulator into something with actual mechanical intrigue. The sound design is doing the heavy lifting for the atmosphere, but the lighting needs a massive overhaul if it wants to compete with modern horror titles.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The lighting lacks any global illumination or bounce. At `01:45.500`, the fluorescent panels are blindingly bright, but the ceiling tiles directly adjacent to them are pitch black. It looks like a 2004 engine test.
2. [hurts] The visual identity is completely generic. The store page mentions a "furniture store"—bring in distorted, impossible IKEA showrooms instead of just relying on the exact same yellow Kane Pixels wallpaper we've seen for years.
3. [hurts] Missing ambient occlusion. Look at the black cubicle dividers at `01:13.500` or the cardboard box at `00:48.500`. They cast no contact shadows on the floor and look like they are floating above the carpet.
4. [nitpick] The ceiling lights (`00:03.000`) look like flat, untextured white emissive stickers. They need some internal structure, a plastic louver grid, or dead bugs so they look like actual physical fixtures.
5. [nitpick] The lighting falloff is completely unnatural. At `00:18.500`, the light from the hallway just stops at a hard, diagonal line on the floor instead of bleeding softly into the dark room.
6. [nitpick] The "DON'T TRUST THE LIGHTS" graffiti at `01:57.500` is a groaner. It's the most cliché indie horror trope imaginable. Let the environment be unsettling on its own without spooky writing telling me how to feel.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder mechanic is brilliant. The way the hidden door glitches and tears into reality on the tape (`00:27.500`) looks incredible and feels snappy to use.
- The sound design is flawless. The squelch of the damp carpet and the heavy, mechanical click of raising the camera are incredibly grounding.
- The subtle impossible geometry, like the unreachable door at `00:56.500`. It makes the space feel actively hostile rather than just empty.

## Steam review one-liner
A tired Backrooms aesthetic saved by a genuinely brilliant camcorder mechanic and suffocating sound design.

## What I want next
I want to see the environment shift entirely away from the generic Backrooms into the distorted, impossible furniture store mentioned in the pitch. Show me puzzles that require me to navigate dead ends using *only* the camcorder screen.
