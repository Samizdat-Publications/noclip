# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I rolled my eyes when I saw the yellow wallpaper—we really do not need another generic Backrooms game. But the camcorder mechanic actually caught me off guard. Having to look through the lens to see the monster, while the naked eye just sees an empty hallway, is a fantastic hook that instantly builds paranoia. That said, visually, it looks incredibly flat right now. If you want this to stand out in a sea of identical liminal space games, the lighting and materials need a massive overhaul to match the strength of your mechanics.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] **Flat, sterile lighting.** In Frame 4, the ceiling lights are just bright white rectangles that don't seem to cast real light. The hallways lack the harsh, sickly contrast that makes liminal spaces scary. It needs deep, dark shadows in the distance and corners, not a smooth gradient of beige.
2. [hurts] **The carpet isn't wet.** The pitch specifically promises "carpet that never dries," but in the video and Frame 3, it looks like a completely dry, matte texture. It needs damp patches, subtle reflections, or squishy footstep sounds to sell that gross, moist feeling.
3. [hurts] **Zero depth on the architecture.** Look at the baseboards and ceiling grids in Frame 3 and Frame 4. They look painted flat onto the walls. Without proper shadowing where the walls meet the floor and ceiling, the environment feels like a cheap 3D model rather than a physical place.
4. [nitpick] **Generic monster design.** The entity in the video is just a tall, dark silhouette. Since we only see it through a glitchy VHS filter, you could get away with something much weirder or more abstract instead of the standard Slenderman shape.
5. [nitpick] **Footsteps lack weight.** The walking sound in the video is a bit generic. It sounds like someone tapping a mic rather than heavy boots sinking into gross, damp industrial carpet.

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic: raising the camcorder to see the invisible monster is a 10/10 idea. Keep it.
- The VHS visual and audio distortion tied to the monster's proximity. It acts as a great natural warning system and obscures the monster just enough to keep it scary.
- The environmental storytelling with the chalk. The "HERE" arrow pointing to a drawn door in Frame 3 is a great, creepy breadcrumb. 

## Steam review one-liner
Looks like another generic Backrooms clone at first glance, but the "invisible monster you can only see through your camcorder" mechanic makes it a uniquely stressful nightmare.

## What I want next
I want to see the camcorder used for more than just spotting the monster—let me use it to find hidden chalk marks, invisible doors, or fake walls to escape the loops. I also desperately want a lighting pass that makes the space look harsh, sickly, and physical.
