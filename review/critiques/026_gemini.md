# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This plays way better than it looks. If I just saw a screenshot, I'd roll my eyes and assume it was yet another generic Backrooms asset flip. The naked-eye environments are incredibly flat and boring. But the moment I realized the monster making that horrible tearing sound was completely invisible until I looked through the camcorder? Pure tension. The audio and that single mechanic completely saved the experience for me.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The lighting is completely flat. Look at frame `00:00.000` or the ceiling in `02:00.500`—the fluorescent lights look like glowing white stickers slapped onto a flat plane. There is no natural light falloff, no bounce, and no dark corners to actually be afraid of. Everything is uniformly illuminated.
2. [hurts] The visual identity is far too generic. It looks exactly like the default Backrooms. The store page mentions a "furniture store"—you need to lean into that. Put some surreal, misplaced furniture or distinct landmarks in there to break up the endless, boring yellow walls.
3. [hurts] Materials lack any physical depth. The wallpaper and ceiling tiles have zero normal mapping or roughness variation. They look like cheap, unlit textures from a 2004 game. 
4. [nitpick] The 133ms frame time spike in `perf.json` means the game hitched for over a tenth of a second on a high-end RTX 5070 Ti laptop. If that stutter happens right when the monster spawns or a room loads, it will completely ruin the tension.

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic of the monster only being visible through the camcorder. It is a fantastic, terrifying idea that forces the player to obscure their own vision to survive.
- The sound design. The squishy, wet carpet footsteps and that violent, tearing paper noise the monster makes are incredibly unsettling.
- The glitchy, stop-motion animation style of the monster when viewed through the tape. It contrasts perfectly with the smooth movement of the player.

## Steam review one-liner
Looks like a generic Backrooms clone, sounds like a nightmare—the camcorder mechanic makes this actually terrifying.

## What I want next
Give me environments that actually look like a twisted, impossible furniture store instead of just empty yellow hallways, and bake some actual shadows and contrast into this lighting.
