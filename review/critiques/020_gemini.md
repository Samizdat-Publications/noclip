# Player Review

## Gut reaction
My first thought was "oh great, another Backrooms asset flip." The visual presentation is incredibly flat, the textures are repetitive, and the lighting feels completely unfinished. However, I have to admit the camcorder mechanic caught me off guard. The moment where I saw a solid wall with my own eyes, but an open doorway through the camera lens, was genuinely brilliant. If you can fix the amateur-hour graphics, you might actually have a solid puzzle-horror game here.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] **Lighting is fundamentally broken.** Look at Frame 4: the fluorescent lights look like glowing white stickers pasted onto a dark ceiling. They cast zero bounce light onto the surrounding tiles, and the volumetric fog cones look like physical objects glued to the fixtures rather than actual light scattering.
2. [ruins it] **Zero material depth.** The yellow wallpaper, the carpet, and the wooden pillars in the big room are completely flat. There is no specular breakup, no normal mapping, and no grime. It looks like a game from 2005. 
3. [hurts] **Generic entity design.** The tall, dark, motionless figure at the end of the hall is the most overused trope in indie horror. The loud static jump-scare teleport (00:10 in video) feels cheap and unearned.
4. [hurts] **No shadow casting.** In the pillar room (Frame 3 and 4), those massive pillars should be casting distinct, overlapping shadows from the ceiling lights. Without them, the room has no depth and the pillars look pasted into the scene.
5. [hurts] **Unjustified performance cost.** Stuttering and dropping into the 40s (fps) on a high-end laptop while rendering basic cubes and flat textures means something is very wrong under the hood. 
6. [nitpick] **Camera lacks physical presence.** When you right-click to bring up the camcorder, the screen just instantly snaps to the filter. It needs a quick animation of hands bringing the device up to your face to ground the mechanic.
7. [nitpick] **Audio is too sparse.** The constant fluorescent hum is fine for the Backrooms vibe, but it needs subtle layers—distant shifting, air vents, or the sound of the carpet squishing—to avoid becoming white noise.

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic of the camcorder revealing hidden geometry (like the door at 0:38) is incredibly strong. Do more of this.
- The concept of finding remnants of previous runs (like the tripod) adds a great layer of environmental storytelling.
- The VHS filter overlay hits the right balance of gritty without making the game unplayable.

## Steam review one-liner
A brilliant camera mechanic trapped inside the body of a 2012 Slenderman clone.

## What I want next
Overhaul the lighting and materials so the environment actually looks like a physical place rather than a texture test map. Give me more complex spatial puzzles using the camcorder, and ditch the static-silhouette jump scares.
