# Player Review

## Gut reaction
The look is strong and consistent: cheap 90s office carpet and wallpaper, huge empty fog-filled pillar halls, and a camcorder overlay that actually adds to the picture. The hero shots (frame_08, frame_15, frame_16) are the kind I'd post. The weak spot is the thing that's supposed to scare me. In frame_02 the creature is a flat black cutout with white speckle on its edge, and it reads as a sprite pasted on the hallway rather than something in the space. I'd keep wandering to see more halls. I'm not sure I'd be scared yet.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] The monster doesn't sit in the world. In frame_02 it's an ink-black cutout with white speckle noise on its edge, with no shading, no contact with the floor and no depth, and the arms are held out stiffly. Across sheet_04 (the 11:20:08 to 11:20:11 stretch) it barely changes pose. I wanted something whose weight and motion I believe, even if I only see it on tape.
2. [hurts] The pillars are the weak object in the hero room. Their tops are notched and ragged (frame_08, frame_14), the columns are uniformly flat yellow, and the base trim is a thin sliver. They look like a stack of identical cardboard tubes, and the repetition shows because every pillar has the same lighting. I wanted some variation in wear, soaking at the base, and a clear join with the ceiling.
3. [hurts] Doorway glow blows out. The lit doorway in frame_05 is a white slab with no detail, and the lit doorway in frame_03 is the same. Where it should pull me in, it looks like a placeholder. I wanted a visible hint of what's through the door instead of pure overexposure.
4. [hurts] There are two different looks for the same space. The unfiltered view (frame_08, frame_12) is bright and fairly cheerful, while the camcorder view (frame_06, frame_17) is darker and grimier. The bright corridor in frame_12 has no dread, and I stopped feeling watched. The dark version, with the red exit glow in frame_03, is the one I wanted all the time.
5. [hurts] The carpet is a flat tan sheet from any distance (frame_08, frame_11, frame_15). Nothing in it says "never dries", and the few dark stains look like decals. I wanted wet patches that catch the light.
6. [nitpick] Odd ghost shapes appear in frame_18, translucent overlapping silhouettes on the pillar and floor on the right beside the mop bucket. I can't tell if this is an intended after-image or a rendering fault. If it's intended, it needs to be much clearer. If it isn't, it needs fixing.
7. [nitpick] The tripod room (frame_07) is lit like a stage with a clean orange spotlight on the back wall. It's pretty, but it reads as a set piece, not a place someone left a camera. The stencil text and VHS tape on the floor are good props, so I'd like that detail everywhere.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder overlay and glitch language: REC dot, battery bars, date stamp, tracking tears, the REW and PLAY screens (frame_19, frame_21), and the door shimmer (frame_10).
- Fog density and falloff in the pillar halls (frame_15, frame_16). The glowing exit sign deep in the haze is a strong focal point.
- Wallpaper detail and stained ceiling tiles close up (frame_09, frame_12).
- The wall-shadow figure (frame_04) and the distant silhouette in a long corridor (frame_01). Both are the monster at its best.
- Performance at 1440p.

## Compared to last build
Mixed, leaning better.
- The pillar hall (prev_06 vs frame_14, frame_15) is better. It was sickly green and is now a warm tan with directional shadows, which looks much more like a place. The ragged pillar tops are the same as before.
- The corridor with the creature (prev_01 vs frame_01) is about the same, so the monster hasn't visibly improved.
- The very dark frames (prev_04) look like they've been fixed. The new room frames (frame_04, frame_16) are readable and keep their mood.
- The tripod room, the chalk door (prev_02/03 vs frame_09/10) and the vignette are the same or slightly clearer.
- The weakest items (monster close-up, door glow, pillar construction) are essentially unchanged.

## Steam review one-liner
Gorgeous tape-glitch backrooms with a genuinely great camcorder, held back by a monster that looks like a black paper cutout. Wishlisted, not yet terrified.

## What I want next
- A monster I believe up close: real shading, weight, and a body that interacts with the light and fog, so I'm scared to film it and not just to run.
- Make the world grimier and more varied: soaked carpet that catches light, pillars that aren't copy-paste, and doorway glows with something visible in them. Hold the darker, red-lit mood of frame_03 and frame_16 as the default.
