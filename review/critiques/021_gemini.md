# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I rolled my eyes when I saw the yellow wallpaper—we've all played a dozen Backrooms clones. But the camcorder mechanic actually got me. Pulling up the viewfinder to see a hallway stretch into the abyss, or realizing you aren't alone *only* when looking through the lens, is a fantastic hook. That said, the base game outside the camera lens looks incredibly flat and unfinished. It feels like two different games: a brilliant analog horror concept trapped inside a barebones engine test.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The lighting in the pillar room (00:53 onwards) is completely flat. It looks like "fullbright" mode is turned on. The ceiling has distinct light squares, but the pillars cast absolutely zero shadows on the floor. It ruins the scale and makes the room look like a blockout map.
2. [hurts] The pitch promises "carpet that never dries," but nothing here looks or sounds wet. The floor texture is a flat, dry noise pattern (00:00), and the footstep audio sounds like walking on a standard office rug. I want squelchy, damp audio and a floor that catches a sickly, wet specular highlight.
3. [hurts] The base graphics without the camcorder are too sterile. The corners where the walls meet the ceiling and floor have no depth or shadowing—they just sharply intersect. It makes the world feel weightless.
4. [nitpick] The ceiling lights in the yellow hallway (00:00) don't actually look like light sources. They are just stark white rectangles without any bloom, glare, or proper light pooling around them on the ceiling tiles.
5. [nitpick] The monster is just a static, tall black silhouette. We've seen Slenderman a million times. If the camera sees things the naked eye can't, give me something truly bizarre or malformed, not just a shadow figure.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder mechanic is gold. The visual contrast between the clean (but boring) real world and the gritty, terrifying VHS world is your strongest asset.
- Leaving evidence of past runs (the dropped tripod at 01:01, the chalk marks) grounds the loop and adds great environmental storytelling.
- The transition from the yellow space to the dark, narrow corridor (00:28) feels seamless and disorienting in the best way.

## Steam review one-liner
It looks like a cheap Backrooms clone until you look through the viewfinder—then it gets under your skin.

## What I want next
Give the base world proper shadows and ambient occlusion so the rooms feel like real physical spaces. Add wet, squishy footstep sounds to sell the "never dries" pitch.
