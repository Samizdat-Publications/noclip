# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It’s another Backrooms game, but it has a genuinely great hook: you have to look through your camcorder to see the monster. That alone creates incredible tension because looking through the camera narrows your vision and adds a layer of grime between you and the world. That said, visually it looks like every other liminal space asset flip on the market right now. If it wants to survive, it has to push the surrealism much harder.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. **[ruins it] The monster is a placeholder.** At 0:07 and 1:16 in the video, the entity is just a static black silhouette. It's the most overused, zero-effort indie horror design out there. Give it a disturbing shape, weird proportions, or an unnatural twitch. 
2. **[hurts] The "wet" carpet is a lie.** The store page promises "carpet that never dries," but looking at Frame 1 and Frame 3, the floor is a flat, matte texture. Add some specular highlights, damp patches, or a slight squish to the footstep audio to sell the dampness.
3. **[hurts] Flat, unconvincing lighting.** In Frame 2, the lighting falloff down the hall is okay, but the lights themselves don't interact with the ceiling grid. There's no volumetric haze catching the light, and the walls lack realistic bounce. It looks like a basic Unity/Unreal tutorial scene.
4. **[hurts] Zero visual identity.** Aside from the chalk marks, this looks exactly like the Kane Pixels Backrooms videos. You need something to make this *your* game. Different wallpaper, bizarre furniture placement, or completely different architecture.
5. **[nitpick] Footstep audio.** The footsteps in the video sound heavy, clunky, and completely dry. They don't match the feeling of walking on a thick, damp hotel carpet.

## Working, don't touch
- The core camcorder mechanic is brilliant. Lowering the camera to walk faster, but risking running blind into the entity, is a top-tier horror loop.
- The VHS glitch transitions (like at 0:10 and 1:30 in the video) are aggressive, loud, and disorienting in the best way possible.
- The environmental storytelling with the chalk marks ("LOOK THROUGH IT", "COPY 31") acts as a great organic tutorial without UI pop-ups.
