# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It's yet another Backrooms game, which normally makes me roll my eyes, but the core camcorder mechanic actually hooked me. Seeing a dead end with your naked eye, then pulling up the camera to reveal a glowing doorway with a tripod waiting inside is a fantastic "oh shit" moment. The normal view looks pretty cheap and flat, but the VHS filter carries the dread perfectly. It has incredible potential, but right now it runs like total garbage on a high-end rig.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] **Performance is abysmal.** You are getting 73 FPS and 1% lows of 54 FPS on an RTX 5070 Ti. For a game with box geometry and simple textures, this is staggeringly bad. I'm guessing the camcorder is rendering the game twice, but you have to optimize this before launch. 
2. [hurts] **Missing contact shadows.** Look at the chair at 00:05.500. Without ambient occlusion or proper shadow casting, it looks like it's hovering two inches off the floor. It completely breaks immersion.
3. [hurts] **The carpet is dry and tiles horribly.** The pitch says "carpet that never dries," but it just looks like flat, dry yellow noise. In the large room (00:54.500) and the pillar room (Still 4), the texture repetition is painfully obvious.
4. [hurts] **Broken visual seam.** At 01:08.500, there is a weird, untextured/metallic vertical strip on the doorway. It doesn't look like a deliberate surreal geometry thing; it looks like a broken mesh or a rendering bug.
5. [nitpick] **Flat normal lighting.** When the camcorder is down (00:01.000, Still 1), the hallways look like a Unity tutorial. The ceiling lights are just bright white rectangles that don't seem to actually cast directional light or interact with the environment.
6. [nitpick] **Footstep audio doesn't match the lore.** If the carpet is supposed to be eternally wet, the footsteps need to sound damp, squishy, or heavy. Right now they just sound like walking on dry felt.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder dual-reality mechanic. Finding chalk marks ("HERE?") and using the lens to find the real path is brilliant.
- The heavy VHS distortion that kicks in right as you walk *through* a hidden door (00:16.500). It makes the impossible space feel physically wrong to enter.
- The UI of the camcorder (the REC, the battery, the date stamp). It grounds the surrealism perfectly.

## Steam review one-liner
Fatal Frame meets the Backrooms—an amazing camcorder mechanic trapped inside a game that desperately needs optimization.

## What I want next
More complex impossible-space puzzles where I have to navigate using *only* the camcorder, and a monster that moves or distorts in a deeply unnatural way instead of just standing there.
