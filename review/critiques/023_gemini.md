# Player Review

## Gut reaction
Oh look, it's the Backrooms again. The yellow wallpaper and damp carpet are exactly what you expect, which means it looks like a dozen other games I've played this year. But the camcorder mechanic actually caught my attention—using it as a lens to see hidden, static-filled doorways over chalk outlines is a really neat trick. The transition from the claustrophobic yellow halls into that massive, foggy pillared room felt great, even if the assets themselves are incredibly basic.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] The lighting in the yellow hallway is totally flat. The fluorescent fixtures don't cast distinct pools of light on the floor or walls, making the whole place look like it has a high ambient light setting rather than actual physical lights.
2. [hurts] The materials are painfully basic. The pillars in the final room (Frame 4) look like untextured white developer blocks, and the yellow wallpaper is way too clean and repetitive.
3. [ruins it] There are weird green and orange color splotches on the ceiling of the pillared room (Frame 4 and the end of the video). It looks like a broken lightmap bake or a corrupted reflection probe, it completely breaks the immersion.
4. [nitpick] The footstep sounds are a bit too rhythmic and fast for the actual movement speed in the video, making it feel like I'm gliding over the carpet rather than stepping on it.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder reveal mechanic. The visual of the static door appearing over the chalk outline (Frame 3) is the best thing in the game.
- The oppressive, deafening hum of the lights. Don't turn it down; it builds the dread perfectly.
- The volumetric fog in the massive pillared room gives a great sense of terrifying scale.

## Steam review one-liner
A genuinely cool camcorder mechanic trapped inside yet another generic Backrooms asset flip.

## What I want next
More impossible geometry and hidden things to find with the camera. Get me out of the standard yellow Backrooms as fast as possible.
