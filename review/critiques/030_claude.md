# Player Review

I could not play the clip.mp4 (no video access), so this is based on frames, sheets, perf.json and the previous stills. Sound is N/A.

## Gut reaction
The backrooms-meets-camcorder look is clean and readable, and the red EXIT room (frame_08) is the first shot that made me want to screenshot something. But I only see about three ideas: yellow hallway, pillar hall, red exit. The monster is still a tiny silhouette at the far end of a hallway. Nearly half of my frames (frame_10 through frame_21, sheet_02, sheet_03) are the same red doorway, so either someone stood still or that spot is boring. It looks good and runs great, but I don't see much horror yet. It's a tech demo of a nice corridor.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The monster has no presence. It is a dark cutout far down the hall in frame_01 and sheet_04, always walking away from camera, with no visible face or readable shape. The store page says it is "far too big for them" and I see no sign of that scale. I want a moment where it fills the corridor, or its shadow, a hand, or a wall crush shows its size.
2. [hurts] The red EXIT scene is overexposed and static. Frames 10, 12, 14, 15, 16, 19, 20 and 21 and sheets 02/03 are one view. The glare behind the door is a washed orange-white blob with no detail (frame_10), and the sign and the lamp inside look like flat props. I wanted a red emergency light that bleeds into the dark with falloff and edge detail, and a reason to stay in this room.
3. [hurts] The corridors are too even and too clean (frame_02, frame_03, frame_12). The wallpaper is identical for 30 m, the light is spread evenly, and there are no stains, water marks or damage. The one stained patch on the left wall of frame_07 shows how much better it looks with grime. I wanted the wet-carpet, never-dries feeling the pitch promises: sheen, dark patches, uneven light.
4. [hurts] The "HALDEN FIELD KIT" locker in frame_12 has huge, thick, outlined text that looks like a UI sticker on a flat box. The work-light stand next to it looks fine. The locker is the first thing the game asks me to interact with, and it is the cheapest-looking object in the build.
5. [hurts] The camcorder effect is inconsistent. The overlay frames (frame_01, frame_04, frame_06) are soft and murky with heavy tracking tears. The clean frames (frame_02, frame_03) are crisp and bright. If the overlay is a toggle, the dark murky version looks better and scarier than the clean one, so I would not want to play in the clean one.
6. [nitpick] The ceiling tiles are the same grid and the same orange "missing tile" holes everywhere (frame_02, frame_03, frame_08, frame_09). The holes read as a stamped pattern, not real damage.
7. [nitpick] The camera room (frame_17) is a closed box with one wall light, a tripod and a crate. It is nicely lit but a very empty set piece. The small VHS tape on the floor is the best part and is barely visible.

## Compared to last build
Mixed, roughly same overall.
- Better: the new red exit lighting (frame_08) and the Halden locker, flare and work-light props (frame_07, frame_12) add colour and things to look at. The pillar hall (frame_08/frame_09) now has hard shadows.
- Worse: the previous build's best stills are not matched here. The foggy deep pillar hall with a lone chair (prev_05) and the dark hall with a glowing exit (prev_06) were more atmospheric. The previous monster shot (prev_01, centered in a corridor, fully visible) was a better look at it than anything I see now. The wall-shadow figure in prev_03 was a creepier idea and is gone.
- Same: the monster is still a distant silhouette, the camera room (prev_02 vs frame_17) is unchanged, and the chalk-door glitch (prev_04 vs frame_06) is unchanged.

## Steam review one-liner
Gorgeous yellow office and a camcorder filter I'd screenshot, but the monster is a speck in the distance and I spent half the footage staring at a red door.

## What I want next
1. Show me the monster up close, lit and big, at least once per run, so "far too big for them" is true.
2. Make the corridors feel wet and wrong (carpet sheen, stains, uneven light), and give the red exit scene more detail and reasons to move.
