# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It’s another Backrooms game, which immediately made me roll my eyes, but the core gimmick actually saved it. Having a camcorder that reveals an alternate reality—showing a monster that isn't there in normal vision, or flipping a pitch-black hallway into a brightly lit one—is a fantastic mechanic. That said, the presentation is currently fighting the concept. The environment feels flat, the audio contradicts the lore, and the monster looks like a placeholder asset. It has a great hook, but it needs a serious coat of polish to stand out from the endless ocean of liminal space clones.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The monster design. In the video (0:08) and Image 1, it just looks like a low-poly default mannequin painted black. When the camera reveals it, I should feel dread, not like I'm looking at a developer test dummy. It needs texture, weird anatomy, or to be obscured by the VHS static.
2. [hurts] The audio/material mismatch. The store page promises "carpet that never dries," but the footstep audio in the video sounds like hard shoes on dry wood or concrete. The carpet texture itself also looks bone-dry. Make it squelch, and add some specular gloss so it looks damp.
3. [hurts] Flat lighting in the normal rooms. In the video at 0:02, the corners where the walls meet the floor and ceiling have absolutely no ambient occlusion or shadow depth. It makes the space look like an unlit 3D model rather than a physical place.
4. [hurts] Instant camera transitions. Pressing the button instantly snaps the screen to the VHS view. It feels like a debug camera toggle. There needs to be a physical animation of raising the camcorder to your face, giving the player a split-second of vulnerability.
5. [nitpick] Performance is weirdly heavy. Averaging 75 FPS on a high-end laptop for a game with mostly bare, flat-lit corridors suggests lighting is rendering through walls or occlusion culling isn't working. 

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic of the camcorder revealing hidden geometry, inverted lighting, and invisible entities. It's incredibly strong.
- The diegetic chalk marks ("LOOK THROUGH IT"). Perfect way to teach the player without a pop-up UI.
- The surreal environmental anomalies, like the fluorescent ceiling light sitting on the floor in Image 2. 
- The single chair facing the wall (Image 4). Classic, unsettling liminal imagery.

## Steam review one-liner
A Backrooms game that actually tries something clever with an alternate-reality camcorder, even if the monster looks like a placeholder.

## What I want next
A redesigned, genuinely terrifying entity, and physical animations for raising/lowering the camcorder to make the mechanic feel grounded.
