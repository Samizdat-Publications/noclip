# Player Review

## Gut reaction
This is a clean, competent Backrooms-style build, and it's the same yellow hallway I've walked a hundred times. The long hall with the fog swallowing the far end (frame_02, frame_14) looks good. The tape-only figure standing at the end of the hall in the camcorder view (frame_01, sheet_04) is the first thing here that made me sit up. But most of what I see is big blank wallpaper, evenly lit rooms, and a single beige-yellow palette from start to finish. Nothing in the stills is a screenshot I'd post. It's solid and a little sleepy. I'd keep playing for the figure and the loop idea, not for the look.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] Everything is the same yellow. Halls, rooms, columns, and corners all share one beige-green-yellow palette (frame_02 through frame_21). The only break is the small orange glow on dead ceiling panels. After five minutes I lose any sense of where I am. I want one or two rooms with a different color temperature, a bad cold fixture, or a wrong-colored wall that really stands out.
2. [hurts] The monster feels like a cutout. In frame_01 and sheet_04 it has jagged edges, a stiff pose, and almost no movement between frames, so it's closer to a mannequin than something watching me. It also sits in a perfectly centered shot. I want it to appear off-center, a little closer each time I look, and to move in ways that make me look away.
3. [hurts] The big blank walls are dead space. Frame_16, frame_17, frame_03, and the big wall in frame_21 are huge flat wallpaper planes with a few vertical stains and nothing to look at. The repeat is too clean and too even. I want stains, tape marks, seams, scuffs, and water damage where the eye lands.
4. [hurts] The lit rooms look evenly filled. Frame_17, frame_18, and frame_19 have bright panels overhead and soft ambient light everywhere, so the rooms feel safe and empty instead of tense. The dark halls (frame_10, frame_12, frame_13) are far more frightening. I'd like the rooms to be more uneven, with one pool of light and the rest falling away.
5. [hurts] The corridor loop and the surreal hook aren't legible. The "the hallway behind you was shorter a minute ago" promise from the store page doesn't show up. Sheet_01 even looks like the same bend repeated. I want one unmistakable "that is wrong" frame.
6. [nitpick] The ceiling panels and orange rims look pasted on. In frame_10 and frame_12 the orange slivers float in the dark ceiling and don't light the tiles around them. A dead tube with an orange rim is a good idea, but it needs to glow onto the nearby tiles and wall. The floor panel in frame_05 is a bright white rectangle sitting on the carpet with a hard edge and almost no spill, so it looks like a light box.
7. [nitpick] Crushed blacks and harsh vignette in the dark frames. In frame_13 the right-hand wall goes fully black, and frame_15 (camcorder) goes muddy dark with a hard bright edge at top left. In a dark horror sequence I want to see some texture in the shadows, so I feel like I'm in a space and not a void.

## Working, don't touch
- The long hall with fog fall-off and receding ceiling panels (frame_02, frame_14). It's the best-looking shot in the set.
- The camcorder overlay and noise bar (frame_01, frame_09, frame_20). It reads as a real device and changes the image feel in a useful way.
- The tape-only figure concept (frame_01, sheet_04). Seeing something only through the lens is a good hook.
- The dark dead-tube hall transition in sheet_02. The slow fade to near-black with a lit room at the far end is the most atmospheric moment I can see.
- The column room (frame_19, sheet_03). It has real scale, and the floor light patches help.
- The chalk graffiti (frame_03, frame_04). Hand-drawn and just readable enough.
- Wallpaper stripe detail and carpet flecks at close range (frame_03, frame_04, frame_16).

## Compared to last build
Mixed, slightly better.
- Better: new chalk graffiti (frame_03, frame_04) and the tape-only figure (frame_01) add story. Dark dead-tube halls (frame_10, frame_12, sheet_02) give much better contrast than the last build's mostly evenly lit rooms (prev_03, prev_05). Pillar rooms read larger (frame_19 vs prev_03).
- Same: the palette. The last build was just as uniformly yellow-green. Its flat blank walls and its dark corner (prev_06) look the same as the blank walls here.
- Lost: the red exit sign over the doorway (prev_04) was the one strong color accent and is absent from this set. The carpet footprints (prev_01, prev_02) are gone from the stills. Both gave the place a human trace, and I'd put them back. The cardboard box (prev_05) was a nice small prop. The new build has chairs but nothing that bleeds story the way the box and footprints did.

## Steam review one-liner
"Pretty, fog-soaked yellow hallways and a great camcorder-only figure, but five minutes in it's all the same beige and I'm waiting for the place to actually lie to me."

## What I want next
1. One or two truly strange rooms or halls that break the yellow, with a different light color, a wrong angle, or a space that clearly can't exist, so I take a screenshot.
2. A better monster presence. It should be off-center, move between glances, and come closer in the camera than in plain view, so the camcorder feels dangerous and not just a filter.
