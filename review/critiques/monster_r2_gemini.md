## My pick to hunt me first, in the yellow rooms
The Light Fixture Spider (1 & 7), because it weaponizes the environment itself to create constant paranoia. In a game about endless identical office rooms, making the player terrified to walk under the ubiquitous ceiling lights is brilliant design. It turns the most mundane, repetitive part of the background into an active threat.

## My pick for deeper down
The Ceiling Light-Tube Ghoul (4 & 5). You said the deeper levels get darker and stranger, and having the only light source in a pitch-black abyss be the glowing chest cavity of a mangled mutant crawling on the ceiling is exactly the kind of surreal, moth-to-a-flame horror that works perfectly down there.

## Which picture sold it
**Light Fixture Spider (1, 7):** Far away on the camcorder (7). In the tight hallway it looks a bit too clean, but in the open room on VHS, it looks like a chaotic, towering tangle of wires and jagged metal that you can't easily outmaneuver.
**Glitchy Office Drones (2, 6):** Far away (6), only because the heavy VHS filter hides how cheap and boring the character models actually look. 
**Giant Hallway Crawler (3, 8):** Close by eye (3). The open room completely ruins the claustrophobia; seeing this massive thing literally scraping the wallpaper off a tight corridor to get to you is pure panic.
**Ceiling Light-Tube Ghoul (4, 5):** Close by eye (4). Having that thing burst through the ceiling tiles right above your head is a great jump scare, whereas it just looks like it's doing zero-gravity yoga in the wide shot.

## Make the winner scarier
1. You shouldn't see it standing in the hallway first; you should walk under a flickering light panel, hear a metallic groan, and watch the fixture violently detach from the ceiling to block your path.
2. It shouldn't scuttle cleanly like a biological spider; it needs to drag its heavy, jagged metal limbs with loud, ear-piercing shrieks of scraping aluminum, accompanied by the deafening hum of a blown transformer.
3. When it catches you, it shouldn't just hit you; it should drop onto your head, wrapping you in wires before the screen blows out into a blinding, buzzing white flash of electrocution.

## Cut
Drop the Glitchy Office Drones (2 & 6). "Faceless men in suits with VHS tracking glitches" is the most overdone, lazy trope in analog horror right now, and I would roll my eyes out of my head if I had to run away from these boring mannequins.
