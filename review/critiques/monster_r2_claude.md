## My pick to hunt me first, in the yellow rooms
The giant crawling man (3, 8). The scale is the horror: a hallway that is too small for it, walls tearing as it squeezes through, hands the length of my torso. The closed eyes make it more disturbing than any snarl would. It reads instantly from far away as "huge naked person, crawling, coming this way", so I understand the threat in half a second and my body reacts before my brain does. It also fits the yellow rooms, because it's a thing that doesn't belong in an office and is too big for it.

## My pick for deeper down
The ceiling hanger (4, 5). It's best when you don't know it's there. The first scare is the moment you look up, so it gets stronger in darker, stranger places where the player is already afraid to check the ceiling. In a dark depth, a pale body lit from within by a tube is scarier than in the bright yellow corridor, where it looks a little too clean and posed.

## Which picture sold it
- **Ceiling hanger:** Scarier by eye (4). The glowing rib cage and the black hole in the ceiling are the whole sell. The camcorder version (5) is more elegant and dreamlike, a floating pale figure, but it feels more like a ghost than a predator. It also reads as a falling or floating figure instead of one hanging and waiting.
- **Row of men:** Scarier on the camcorder (6). The suits, the pixel-eaten faces and the long row in the big hall give it a real "this is wrong" feeling. The close version (2) looks like a character-select screen.
- **Light-fixture spider:** Scarier on the camcorder (7). The tilted lit fixture, the wires and the vague leg shapes behind pillars feel like something you shouldn't have filmed. By eye (1) the legs are tidy grey metal and the whole thing looks like a clean Halloween prop, which kills the dread.
- **Giant crawling man:** Scarier by eye (3). The hand and the torn wallpaper in the narrow corridor carry it. The camcorder (8) is a bit too calm; it looks like a muscular statue stretching out in a big room, so the scale and the claustrophobia are lost.

## Make the winner scarier
For the giant crawling man, my pick to hunt me first:
1. **How I first see it:** Don't give me the full body. First I hear a slow dragging sound and feel carpet and ceiling tiles shift. Then I see only a hand slide into the end of a corridor, then the shoulder scraping the wall, then the eyes closed against the ceiling. Let the player work out the scale. Let the camcorder light catch the closed face before the body, so my first reaction is "it's asleep" and the second is "no, it's tracking me by sound".
2. **How it moves:** Slow and patient most of the time, with heavy, wet, deliberate crawling and brief unnatural bursts of speed. It should never be fast for long, but every burst has to feel like it breaks something (a wall panel, a column, a door). It tracks sound, so if I run it speeds up and if I freeze it slows, and it presses its face to the ground to listen. When it can't fit through a gap it squeezes anyway, and the room warps around it.
3. **What it sounds like and what it does when it catches me:** The sound is slow, wet joint cracks, plaster tearing, and a faint breathing through the nose, like someone sleeping. There's no roar. When it catches me it doesn't hit me. It slowly pins me with one huge hand, the eyes stay closed, and the face leans in until the screen fills with it. Then the eyelids open, the player sees what's behind them, and it cuts to black. That ending is what gets people to rewatch the death.

## Cut
The row of men (2, 6). It's the weakest on every count that matters here. It's the least original, since "identical people in a line" is a familiar creepypasta idea. It's the least threatening, because it's just standing there, and it's the hardest to make chase or catch you in a way that's scary the tenth time. The glitching dissolve is nice, but that's an effect, not a monster. If the team wants to keep anything, keep the dissolve-into-static effect as a way to hide or reveal the other creatures.
