# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It’s yet another Backrooms-inspired game, but the central mechanic actually hooked me. Having to look through the camcorder's tiny, pixelated screen to see the monster while your naked eyes see an empty room is a genuinely terrifying concept. Unfortunately, the raw visuals outside the camcorder look incredibly cheap right now. The lighting is totally flat and lifeless, making it feel more like an early gray-box prototype than a finished atmospheric horror game. If they can make the environments look as good as the camera gimmick feels, it could be a sleeper hit.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The lighting is completely flat. In Frame 1, the ceiling lights look like white stickers—they aren't glowing, there's no bloom, and they don't seem to be actually casting the light onto the walls.
2. [hurts] Zero ambient occlusion. Look at the pillars in Frame 2 and Frame 3; there are no contact shadows where they meet the floor or the ceiling, making them look like unmoored gray blocks dropped into the scene.
3. [hurts] The monster's audio doesn't match the lore. The store page says it's "made of the same paper as the walls," but in the video (around 1:30), it just sounds like generic digital glitching and static. I wanted to hear dry, unsettling paper rustling and tearing in the dark.
4. [hurts] The materials are too clean. The yellow wallpaper in the opening halls and the gray pillars in the massive room are perfectly smooth. They need some normal mapping, smudges, or roughness variation to feel like a physical place.
5. [nitpick] The shadows in the yellow hallways (visible in the first 10 seconds of the video) are razor-sharp and pitch black, which isn't how fluorescent light scatters in a small corridor. 

## Working, don't touch
- The core mechanic of the monster only being visible through the camcorder screen. It forces the player to limit their field of view to survive, which is excellent design.
- The UI and visual effects on the tape. The "END OF TAPE" rewind sequence at the end of the video is incredibly stylish and a great way to handle a death screen.
- The duct tape door outline visible in Frame 4. It looks exactly like the pitch described and stands out perfectly through the lens.
