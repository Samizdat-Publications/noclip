# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It's a competent yellow-office liminal game that looks clean and runs great. It doesn't scare me yet. Everything is lit evenly, so the dread never builds. The best moments are the VHS camera view and the taped-over doorway that glitches into a tripod room (frame_05, frame_11). The worst is the wallpaper-skin monster, which looks like a mannequin once the camera is down (frame_18). It's a 6 as a build and a 5 as horror. Last build's shadows and fog had more mood, and I miss them.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] The lighting is flat and over-fair. Every room is filled with the same soft yellow wash, so I can see everything and nothing is hidden (frame_04, frame_12, frame_19, frame_20). I wanted pools of light with real black between them, and a corner I'm afraid to walk past. A horror game shouldn't feel like a furniture showroom after hours.
2. [hurts] The monster stops working in plain view. In frame_18 the figure is a lit, tan mannequin in a goofy pose, and in frame_16 it's a crouching stick figure. Only the dropout look on tape sells it. I wanted it to stay wrong, glimpsed, and hard to fully see, with nothing like a dancing dummy standing in the open.
3. [hurts] The big hall is empty and washed out (frame_19, frame_20). The columns are pale beige, nearly the same colour as the wall. The ceiling is bright, flat and fogged. Last build's cold, foggy column hall (prev_06) had far more scale and dread. This one reads as an unfinished warehouse.
4. [hurts] The repetition is obvious. frame_01, frame_02 and frame_04 are the same hallway: same doors, same crack on the right wall, same bulb spacing. Frame_04 is also the same room shape as frame_16. Walking it will feel copy-pasted, and I notice in stills already.
5. [hurts] The props are thin. The brown box desk (frame_04), the plain white dresser (frame_16), the slab door (frame_15) and the water cooler (frame_13) are readable but plastic. Nothing in the rooms tells a story, so the rooms feel staged instead of lived in and abandoned.
6. [nitpick] The ceiling has clean, bright tile and bare light panels with no flicker or dead tubes visible (frame_03, frame_09). Stains and the odd dark tile help (frame_16, frame_19), but there should be more broken lights, so the light itself can threaten me.
7. [nitpick] The hallway far end is a milky haze wall (frame_01, frame_02, frame_09). It reads as a fog sprite instead of distance. I wanted real darkness there, or something standing in it.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder look: the overlay, the date stamp, the tracking noise, the vignette and the battery bars (frame_10, frame_14).
- The chalk-door gag: a doorway outline on the wall that reads as a real room with a tripod on tape (frame_05, frame_11).
- The embossed wallpaper, with its strong chevron relief and creases (frame_01, frame_16), and the ceiling tiles with water stains (frame_16, frame_19).
- The surreal single-door placements: the high floating door (frame_15) and the knee-high doorway (frame_17).
- Performance. Do not trade it away.

## Compared to last build
Mixed, and I lean slightly worse for mood.
- Better: the camcorder is now on screen with a flip-out display (sheet_01). The figure matches the wallpaper and glitches on tape, instead of the pale fleshy humanoid at the hallway end (previous sheet). The tape and door image is cleaner (frame_05 vs prev_05). Nothing is clipped to pitch black or blown white anymore (prev_03, prev_04).
- Worse: the contrast and mood. Last build had a dark hallway with warm light pools (prev_01) and a cold foggy pillar hall (prev_06). This build is a bright, uniform yellow everywhere. The dark and white-blown extremes are gone, but so are the shadows and tension that made them work. The pillar hall has gone from my favourite image to the most generic one (frame_19 vs prev_06).
- Same: the hallway and the chalk-door wall (frame_16 vs prev_02) look much the same.

## Steam review one-liner
Clean, smooth backrooms-style office with a great camcorder look and a good door gag, but it's lit so evenly there's nowhere for the dread to hide yet.

## What I want next
- Make the lighting scary: dark stretches, a few dying or dead tubes, and one hallway where I can only see a few steps ahead, so the camera's view and the real view feel like different places.
- Fix the monster's visibility. Keep it only on the tape or at the very edge, never lit and standing around. Bring back last build's cold, foggy, high-contrast pillar hall, with the Occupant in it.
