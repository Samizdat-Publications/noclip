# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I'm honestly torn. The core mechanic of only seeing the monster and hidden messages through the camcorder is brilliant, and the audio is genuinely panic-inducing. But the actual naked-eye graphics look like a 2012 Unity tutorial. It's another Backrooms game, which is already a tired setting, and the complete lack of basic rendering features like contact shadows makes the environment feel cheap instead of liminal. I love the paranoia the camera creates, but the visual execution needs a massive overhaul.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] Absolutely zero Ambient Occlusion. Look at the pillars in Frame 3 and Frame 4, or the corners of the walls in Frame 2. There are no contact shadows where objects meet the floor or ceiling, making the entire world look like floating, unanchored geometry. 
2. [hurts] The materials are completely flat. The ceiling grid in Frame 2 isn't modeled, it's just a flat texture with lines drawn on it. The yellow wallpaper desperately needs a normal map to give it that tactile, gross paper feel you mentioned in the pitch.
3. [hurts] The naked-eye lighting is washed out and boring. Frame 2 has almost no directional lighting or contrast. It just looks like uniform ambient light was cranked up to 100%. 
4. [hurts] Performance stutters. Getting a 134ms frame spike and 1% lows of 32 FPS on a high-end laptop is crazy for a game with graphics this simple. It noticeably hitched in the video around the respawn/teleport sequence.
5. [nitpick] The monster's run animation. At 0:18 in the video, the abstract, terrifying wireframe entity suddenly just looks like a generic humanoid doing a standard sprint animation. It loses all its otherworldly menace the second it moves.

## Working, don't touch
- The sound design is carrying the entire game right now. The deafening hum and the sickening squish of the carpet are perfectly dialed in.
- The camcorder mechanic. Seeing "LOOK THROUGH IT" on the wall (0:06) only when looking through the lens is a fantastic way to build paranoia.
- The snappy speed of raising and lowering the camcorder. It feels responsive, which is vital if I have to keep checking it to survive.

## Steam review one-liner
A brilliant camcorder mechanic and terrifying audio trapped inside a game that looks like a high schooler's first rendering project.

## What I want next
Turn on Ambient Occlusion, give the walls and ceiling some actual texture depth, and fix the monster's goofy running animation.
