# Player Review

## Gut reaction
I'm torn. On one hand, this looks like yet another generic Backrooms clone with the exact same yellow wallpaper and flat lighting we've seen a hundred times on itch.io. On the other hand, the camcorder mechanic is genuinely awesome. Using the viewfinder to reveal a real, glitching door inside a chalk outline is a fantastic AR-style puzzle hook. If the whole game leans into this impossible-space lens mechanic it could be a sleeper hit, but right now the base environments look too cheap and derivative to grab attention.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [hurts] **The wallpaper tiling is egregious.** In Image 2 and the wide shots of the grid, the yellow wallpaper pattern repeats so uniformly it looks like a 2004 source engine map. It desperately needs grime, uneven fading, or larger texture variations to break up the endless tiling.
2. [hurts] **The carpet isn't wet.** The store page pitches "carpet that never dries," but in every frame, it looks like a flat, dry noise texture. It needs specularity, damp dark patches, or gross squishy footprints to sell that miserable, damp feeling.
3. [ruins it] **The office partitions look unfinished.** In Image 3 and frame 00:01:13.500, the black room dividers look like completely untextured primitive blocks. They stand out terribly against the rest of the room.
4. [hurts] **Harsh lighting cutoffs.** In frame 00:00:18.500, the transition into the dark room is just a harsh, straight line of pitch black. It doesn't feel like a scary dark room; it feels like the lighting engine just stopped rendering. We need some light bounce or volumetric fog to make the dark spaces feel physical.
5. [nitpick] **Lack of visual identity.** Without the camcorder raised, a screenshot of this game (Image 2) is entirely indistinguishable from every other Backrooms game on the market. It needs a unique visual signature in the "normal" view.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder reveal mechanic (Image 4 / 00:00:27.500). The chalk outline glitching into a physical door through the viewfinder is brilliant and looks fantastic.
- The distorted tripod shadow in frame 00:00:31.500. It stretches unnaturally up the wall and ceiling, creating an incredibly eerie, subtle scare without resorting to a monster.
- The UI of the camcorder. The battery indicator, timecode, and subtle curvature sell the retro hardware feel perfectly without obscuring too much vision.

## Steam review one-liner
A genuinely brilliant camera mechanic trapped inside a painfully generic Backrooms asset flip.

## What I want next
More impossible-space puzzles using the camera (like walkways that only exist through the lens), and a serious texture pass on the walls and floors to make the space feel gross and lived-in.
