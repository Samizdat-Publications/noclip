# Player Review

## Gut reaction
It's yet another Backrooms game, but the core gimmick is actually fantastic. Using the camcorder to reveal invisible doors and track a monster you can't see with your naked eye creates incredible tension, and the oppressive audio design backs it up perfectly. It's just a shame that the moment you actually get a good look at the monster, all the fear evaporates because it looks like a goofy arts-and-crafts project. If they fix the flat lighting and redesign the creature, this could be a standout.

## Top problems (ranked, max 7)
1. [ruins it] The monster design and animation. In the video (0:18) and Frame 3, it looks like a low-poly papier-mâché stick figure doing a stiff waddle. It completely kills the incredible tension built by the audio. I wanted something abstract, shadowy, or deeply unsettling, not a geometry class project.
2. [hurts] The lighting is entirely flat. Frame 1 shows a long hallway where the ceiling lights don't actually cast pools of light or create stark contrast. The whole room is just uniformly illuminated, which makes it feel like an old GMod map rather than a modern horror game.
3. [hurts] Missing contact shadows. In Frame 3, the monster looks like it was pasted onto the screen in Photoshop because there is zero shadowing connecting its feet to the floor. 
4. [nitpick] The carpet doesn't look wet. The store page pitches "carpet that never dries," but the dark patches just look like dirt. It needs some specular reflection or roughness adjustments so it actually catches the ceiling lights like damp fabric would.
5. [nitpick] Lack of ambient occlusion. The seams where the walls meet the floor and ceiling (visible in Frame 1 and 4) are completely flat, lacking the dark corners that give a room depth and realism.

## Working, don't touch
- The camcorder mechanic. Being forced to restrict your FOV and look through a grainy lens to see the true environment is a massive win.
- The sound design. The heavy, thumping footsteps and the oppressive room tone do 90% of the heavy lifting for the game's horror.
- The portal transitions. Walking through a blank wall because the camera shows a door there feels incredibly smooth and satisfying.

## Steam review one-liner
Fatal Frame meets the Backrooms, but the ghost is made of cardboard and tape.

## What I want next
A complete visual overhaul of the monster, and a lighting pass that makes the environments feel dark, contrast-heavy, and genuinely threatening when the camera is down.
