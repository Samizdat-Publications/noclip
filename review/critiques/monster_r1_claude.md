## The one that hunts me in the first yellow rooms
1. Option 7: A giant crawling thing that fills the whole corridor means there is no side to dodge to, and its calm face is the creepiest part. It's the one I'd stop and back away from.
2. Option 9: The tape format and the distance make it the most believable and the hardest to read. It's the scare that comes with the camcorder instead of fighting it.
3. Option 3: The one I'd least expect to be scared of, since they just calmly walk toward me. A hallway full of the same man is a perfect first-rooms creep, because nothing about it announces itself as a monster.

## Deeper down
Option 8 belongs in the big pillared hall levels. Open space and a ceiling hatch let it hang over you where you can't see. The upside-down body and the tube-light spine fit a place where the rules are stranger.
Option 5 or option 2 belong deeper too. They are both made of the building's own stuff (carpet, furniture) and only work after the player has accepted that the place is alive. The chair one is only scary if it moves wrong and fast, so it needs the weird-level framing around it. Option 6 would also fit down there, as a ceiling thing that hunts by the lights.

## Make them scarier
**Option 7 (crawler):**
- Never show the whole thing until the second time. First glimpse is a hand at the edge of a doorway, then the shoulder dragging against the wall, then the head tilted in the corridor behind you. Sound: skin squeaking along wallpaper and a slow wet breath, with silence when it stops.
- Movement: the crawl should be too smooth, with no visible effort, and the head should stay tilted and eyes closed until it is almost on you, then open. It should be fast in a straight line and stuck at corners, so the player learns to turn corners.

**Option 9 (tape figure):**
- Only ever see it on camcorder. Take it off the camera and it is gone, or it was never there in the room. It should be closer in every look, with no walking in between. Cut to it nearer on the tape's frame skips.
- Sound: tape warble and a faint dry click on every skipped frame. Let it lunge during a skip, so the player thinks it teleported.

**Option 3 (queue of men):**
- Keep the faces blurred and never let one turn around. The last one in the line should be the one at the front of the line next time, and the player should realize there's one more of them every time.
- Sound: perfectly synchronized footsteps, one set. If I stop, they stop in step, and one of them keeps walking a fraction of a second longer. That small break is the scare.

## Laugh or refund
- Option 1: The block-head wallpaper golem would make me laugh. It reads as a cheap Roblox-style mob and I'd stop being afraid of it within seconds.
- Option 2: The chair caterpillar teeters on the line. If it moves stiff or fast with a squeak I'd laugh and quit. Done with real dread, it works. A spec-sheet chair that rolls at me is a joke.
- Option 4: Not a laugh, but a shrug. If I've seen it before I won't be scared by it.
